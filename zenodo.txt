Description:
This deposit contains the complete analysis pipeline, raw results, and
manuscript source for Paper 4 of the Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP)
GNSS series. The paper presents a strict held-out replication test using
the public MGEX combined multi-GNSS receiver-clock product (CODE COD0MGXFIN)
from NASA CDDIS / IGS for 2025–2026. The product provides a single combined
receiver-clock solution per station; per-constellation separation is not
possible from these files. All six predictions were frozen before the
held-out data were inspected.

Abstract:
The Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) predicts correlated phase-coherent
disturbances in GNSS timekeeping with a spatial correlation length of order
thousands of kilometres, an east–west anisotropy exceeding the north–south
counterpart, coupling to Earth orbital velocity, and a preferred axis near the
CMB rest frame. Papers 1–3 established these signatures in GPS-only precise point
positioning (PPP) products and raw RINEX observations. This paper presents a
held-out replication using the public MGEX combined multi-GNSS receiver-clock
product (CODE COD0MGXFIN) from NASA CDDIS / IGS for 2025–2026. Receiver-clock
offsets for 332 globally distributed stations are read directly from the daily
5-minute CLK files; no positioning is performed. Four of the five evaluated
signatures are recovered: λ = 2429 ± 323 km (R² = 0.501, 2.79 million pairs);
EW/NS anisotropy emerges in the longitude-matched subset (ratio 5.28, p < 0.001);
a supplementary PA-difference metric recovers orbital-velocity coupling (r = −0.672,
p = 0.017); and the signal persists on geomagnetically quiet days and passes all
four null tests. The CMB-frame test detects a significant anisotropy axis
(LEE p < 0.0001) at RA = 10°, Dec = −30° whose antipode lies 43° from the CMB
dipole, consistent with an ionospheric origin.
