2. Data and Product Scope

2.1 MGEX Combined Clock Product

All data are public and freely available from NASA CDDIS (cddis.nasa.gov) and the International GNSS Service (IGS). The analysis uses the Multi-GNSS Experiment (MGEX) combined receiver-clock product COD0MGXFIN produced by the Center for Orbit Determination in Europe (CODE), with the Wuhan (WUM) and CNES (GRG) MGEX products as fallbacks on the few days CODE is unavailable (WUM 20 days, GRG 9 days out of 486 calendar days in the window, i.e. ≈6 % fallback usage). These are daily clock (CLK) files at a 5-minute sampling interval. Each file reports a single combined multi-GNSS receiver-clock estimate per station, derived by CODE from GPS, GLONASS, Galileo and BeiDou observations; the combined solution is the quantity analysed. No positioning is performed and no raw RINEX observation files are used.

2.2 Analysis Window and Station Set

The analysis period is 2025-01-01 to 2026-05-01 (462 days with usable data out of 486 calendar days in the window), yielding approximately 16 months of daily files. The window is chosen to provide a largely held-out 2025–2026 epoch, though it is not fully temporally disjoint from Papers 1–2, which span 2000–2025. Receiver-clock offsets are extracted from the AR (receiver-clock) records of each CLK file for 418 globally distributed stations, converted to nanoseconds, and assembled into per-station daily time series.

2.3 Product Limitation: No Per-Constellation Separation

Unlike Papers 1–3, which can isolate individual systems, the MGEX CLK product contains one combined receiver clock per station rather than per-constellation clocks. The four constellations therefore cannot be separated in this product, and the analysis is performed once on the combined solution. The independence offered here is temporal (a largely held-out epoch) and product-type (a combined multi-GNSS clock product line), not per-constellation and not analysis-centre: COD0MGXFIN is produced by CODE, the same centre whose IGS-final products anchor Papers 1 and 2.

Agreement with Papers 1–2 is therefore corroboration within a shared product ecosystem — a different product line and a largely held-out epoch over substantially the same tracking network — rather than replication on independent data. The GRG and WUM products used for fallback and matched-day comparison run different processing software on that same network, so they bound software- and implementation-specific artifacts but not network-level or datum-level common modes. The estimation-chain-independent axis in the corpus is Paper 3 (raw RINEX, broadcast ephemerides, own processing); Paper 33's broadcast-SPP arms provide a second such axis, while its analysis-centre arms share this ecosystem.

2.5 Evaluated Signatures

The analysis evaluates the following signatures, consistent with the approach in earlier papers: correlation length, azimuthal anisotropy, orbital-velocity coupling, annual-phase directional structure, fixed-frame controls, ionospheric independence, and geometric robustness.
