6. Conclusions

This product-type independence stress test uses the CODE COD0MGXFIN combined multi-GNSS receiver-clock product from 2025-01-01 to 2026-05-01. Its principal result is a distance-dependent correlation scale of $$\lambda = 1862 \pm 112$$ km (R² = 0.699), stable across the tested baseline cutoffs and present on quiet geomagnetic days. This is cross-product corroboration of a spatial structure at the thousands-of-kilometres scale reported in the earlier TEP-GNSS papers, while remaining subject to the different metric and combined-clock product construction.

The secondary results are deliberately qualified. Standard anisotropy and primary orbital metrics are not robust in this short MGEX interval. A model-free PA-difference statistic has strong associations with orbital speed (r = −0.716, p = 0.0018) and CMB-projected orbital velocity (r = −0.767, p = 0.0005) across 16 chronological months. Because those signs were anticipated by previous TEP-GNSS work, the result is corroborative across products; because this operationalisation was selected after primary MGEX fits were unstable, it remains exploratory within this study and needs a prospective, longer-baseline replication.

The CMB-projected orbital association is likewise consistent: the direct monthly CMB projection has r = −0.733, while the weighted PA-difference test has r = −0.767 (p = 0.0005). The grid’s nominal RA = 230°, Dec = +60° maximum is 82.7° from the CMB dipole, but this is not contrary evidence because orbital velocity constrains only an axis’s ecliptic-plane projection. The grid cannot uniquely localize a three-dimensional sky direction; its within-grid permutation statistic (plus-one p = 0.005 from 200 shuffles) measures annual directional structure. The satellite-clock analysis is limited by the combined product’s shared reference time and does not recover an exponential signal. The next decisive test is a preregistered chronological analysis over multiple annual cycles, reporting an identifiable plane-projected direction or using non-coplanar velocity information.
