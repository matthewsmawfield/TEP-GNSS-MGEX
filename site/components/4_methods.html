3. Methods

3.1 Receiver-Clock Extraction

For each daily CLK file the AR records are parsed, which report the combined multi-GNSS receiver-clock offset for each station at the 5-minute sampling interval. The offsets are converted from seconds to nanoseconds and ordered in time, giving one clock time series per station per day. No positioning is performed and no coordinate residuals are computed; the receiver clock is the sole observable.

3.2 Spectral Pre-processing

Each daily series is linearly detrended to remove the dominant clock drift. Cross- and auto-spectra are estimated with Welch's method (Hann window, segment length up to 96 samples). Spectral estimates are restricted to the nominal TEP-sensitive band [10 µHz, 500 µHz], but with the daily Welch segmentation used here the effective lowest non-zero resolved frequency is set by the segment length, approximately 35 µHz for 96 five-minute samples. The practical analysed band is therefore approximately 35–500 µHz, corresponding to periods from about 8 hours down to 33 minutes, which lies safely within the 5-minute Nyquist limit.

3.3 Spectral Phase-Alignment Metric

For every station pair, the magnitude-weighted circular mean of the cross-spectral phase is computed across the selected frequency band. The phase-alignment index is the cosine of the weighted mean phase, ranging from −1 (anti-phase) to +1 (in-phase). It measures signed mean phase-offset alignment, not phase concentration; the latter requires the magnitude of the complex resultant. The index is bounded and saturating — it reports the directional dominance of the in-phase component of the band-integrated cross-spectrum rather than the correlated-power fraction — so its fitted exponential amplitude is a phase-direction measure, not a calibrated coupling, and is decomposed against a linear coherence estimator in §4.5b. The established phase-alignment statistic is retained consistently with the earlier GNSS analyses, and is the quantity carried through all subsequent tests. Pairs are binned by great-circle distance into 40 logarithmically spaced bins from 50 km to 13,000 km, with a minimum of 10 pairs per bin.

3.4 Correlation-Length Model

The distance-binned phase alignment is modelled as

$$C(r) = A \exp(-r / \lambda) + C_0 \tag{1}$$

where A is the amplitude, λ is the correlation length, and C₀ is an incoherent offset. Fitting uses bounded non-linear least squares (Trust Region Reflective), weighting each bin by the inverse of its standard error of the mean (1/SEM, where SEM = σ_bin / sqrt(count)). Fit quality is quantified by R², and the uncertainty on λ is propagated from the covariance matrix. Because pair-day records share stations within each day and orbit/product states across consecutive days, the formal covariance error is supplemented by cluster-robust resampling of whole epochs: a day-block bootstrap, a calendar-month-block bootstrap, and a leave-one-month-out jackknife, each refitting Eq. (1) on the resampled record pool. The choice of morphology is likewise tested rather than assumed: on identical bins and weights the exponential is compared by AIC against a stretched exponential with free exponent, the algebraic family A/[1+(r/r_t)^k]+C₀ with free k and at the derived Paper-0 pairwise exponents k = 4 and k = 4/3, and a Gaussian. Sensitivity rows at raised minimum-baseline cutoffs are flagged when the fitted λ contacts the lower parameter bound (which equals the cutoff), since a boundary-pinned value is not an interior measurement.

3.5 Anisotropy, Orbital, CMB, Ionospheric, and Null Tests

The EW > NS test fits λ separately in eight 45°-wide azimuth sectors and compares the mean of the east and west sectors with the mean of the north and south sectors. Because ionospheric decorrelation inverts the anisotropy at long baselines, a longitude-matched subset is also reported (station-pair longitude difference below 30°), which, following Paper 3, isolates pairs at similar local solar time. Significance and confidence intervals for the ratio are obtained from 500 bootstrap resamples. For anisotropy, pair-bootstrap results are reported as sensitivity tests, while station/spatially-clustered resampling is treated as the more conservative inference because station pairs are not independent. The ratio is additionally reported in three distance strata (0–500, 500–1,000, >1,000 km).

The orbital-coupling test correlates the monthly λ and the monthly EW/NS ratio with the projection of Earth's orbital velocity onto the CMB dipole direction, computed from a sinusoidal annual model; the two correlations are Bonferroni-corrected. Because exponential-fit noise on short baselines can degrade these metrics, an exploratory PA-difference metric is also computed: the monthly mean EW minus NS phase alignment, which avoids fitting entirely and directly measures anisotropy strength. The physical hypothesis and sign were motivated by earlier TEP-GNSS analyses, but this operationalisation was selected after the primary MGEX estimators proved unstable; it is therefore exploratory within MGEX and excluded from confirmatory family-wise inference. Because the 16-month series is short and its monthly values are plausibly autocorrelated, the nominal Gaussian p-values for the exploratory PA-difference associations are accompanied by four robustness diagnostics computed in the same step: an effective-degrees-of-freedom correction from the residual lag-1 autocorrelation (n_eff = n(1−ρ̂)/(1+ρ̂), t-test on n_eff−2), a Monte-Carlo ensemble of AR(1) surrogates matched to the observed residual autocorrelation, a phase-preserving circular-shift permutation of the monthly series against the fixed periodic predictor (resolution floor 1/n), and a leave-one-month-out correlation range testing whether a single phase alignment dominates. Two operational-asymmetry controls further price the vulnerability of the raw EW − NS contrast to unequal east/west station populations: a composition-matched difference computed as the equal-weight mean of cell-level EW − NS contrasts over (distance × |midpoint-latitude|) cells, and a uniformly-tracked-station subset restricted to pairs whose two endpoints both sit above the median pair-day participation count; each variant is correlated with the same two predictors under the same diagnostics. Months are retained chronologically (YYYY-MM) and a partial trailing month is excluded. The CMB-frame test performs a full-sky grid search for the anisotropy axis on a 37 × 19 grid in right ascension and declination (10° steps), correlating the monthly EW−NS phase-alignment difference with the projection of Earth's orbital velocity onto each candidate axis. The difference metric is used in place of the EW/NS ratio because phase alignment ranges from −1 to +1, making the ratio unstable when the NS mean is near zero; the difference is bounded and requires no sign-based selection filter. Monthly aggregation reduces daily noise, while the daily analysis is retained as a supplementary diagnostic. The grid search tracks both the most positive and most negative correlation extrema. Because orbital velocity samples essentially the ecliptic plane, the scan is interpreted as a test of a plane-projected annual predictor, not as a unique three-dimensional axis localization; a nominal sky-angle threshold is reported descriptively only. A permutation null performs a complete grid search over all 703 directions for each of 200 permutations, accounting for the look-elsewhere effect directly. Ionospheric controls stratify the fit by geomagnetic Kp index and exclude storm days; geometry controls repeat the fit on hemisphere-balanced and distance-matched subsets; a forward-modelled product-error kernel tests the shared satellite-error projection channel directly; hemisphere-class and latitude-band within-product partitions test the weather-organized atmospheric channel; and four null tests (temporal shuffle, spatial shuffle, phase randomisation, and solar-rotation reassignment) test whether the recovered structure vanishes under label permutation.

3.6 Sensitivity and Robustness Analysis

Because the 418-station network contains dense regional clusters that disproportionately populate short baselines, a sensitivity analysis is performed to verify that the reported results are not artifacts of short-baseline fitting noise. For each primary result the analysis is repeated at five minimum-baseline cutoffs (50, 200, 500, 1,000, and 2,000 km), tabulating the correlation length, anisotropy ratio, and CMB correlation as functions of the cutoff. A robust supplementary analysis uses a 500-km minimum baseline and a 50-pair minimum bin count, eliminating bins with fewer than 50 pairs where small-sample fluctuations can produce extreme coherence means. For the anisotropy ratio a station-declustered point estimate is also computed, weighting each pair by the inverse square root of the product of its two endpoints' pair counts so that stations in dense clusters do not dominate the exponential fit. The robust null tests apply the same elevated thresholds to all four shuffle controls. These sensitivity analyses do not replace the pre-specified primary analysis but provide a conservative supplementary assessment of each result's stability.
