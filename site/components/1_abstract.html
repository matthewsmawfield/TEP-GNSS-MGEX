Abstract

The Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) predicts correlated phase-coherent disturbances in GNSS timekeeping with a spatial correlation length of order thousands of kilometres, an east–west anisotropy, coupling to Earth orbital velocity, and a preferred axis near the CMB rest frame. Papers 1 and 2 examined these signatures in GPS precise point-positioning products from three analysis centres spanning 2000–2025; Paper 3 used raw RINEX single-point positioning.

This paper is a product-type independence stress test using the public CODE COD0MGXFIN combined multi-GNSS receiver-clock solution distributed by NASA CDDIS from 2025-01-01 to 2026-05-01. Receiver-clock offsets for 418 globally distributed stations are read directly from daily 5-minute CLK files. The combined solution provides no per-constellation independence, but supplies a distinct product family and a largely held-out 2025–2026 epoch.

The isotropic correlation length is $$\lambda = 1862 \pm 112$$ km (R² = 0.699; 1.83 million pair-day observations). It is stable at 1,819–2,000 km across minimum-baseline cutoffs of 50–2,000 km and persists on geomagnetically quiet days ($$\lambda = 1708$$ km). The full-range anisotropy is not robust (ratio 1.29, station-clustered p = 0.49); the longitude-matched subset is suggestive by pair bootstrap but not spatially clustered resampling. The primary monthly $$\lambda$$ and EW/NS orbital metrics are null (Bonferroni p > 0.5). A model-free PA-difference statistic gives associations with orbital speed (r = −0.716, p = 0.0018) and CMB-dipole velocity projection (r = −0.767, p = 0.0005) across 16 chronological full months. The physical hypothesis and sign were motivated by earlier TEP-GNSS papers, making this cross-product corroboration; the operationalisation remains exploratory within MGEX because it was selected after the primary estimators were unstable. A 37 × 19 directional grid finds strong within-grid modulation (200-permutation plus-one p = 0.005). Its nominal maximum is 82.7° from the CMB dipole, but this does not contradict the CMB result: orbital velocity constrains only an axis’s projection onto the orbital plane, and that projection is highly degenerate. The direct CMB-projection association is therefore consistent but not a unique three-dimensional axis localization. All four null controls pass under a robust analysis. A supplementary satellite-clock analysis does not recover the exponential structure, consistent with the shared reference-time architecture of the combined MGEX product.
