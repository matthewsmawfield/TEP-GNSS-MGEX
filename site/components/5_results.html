4. Results

The analysis comprises 1,827,555 station-pair-day observations drawn from a network of 418 stations over 462 usable days. Because the MGEX product is a single combined solution, the results are reported once rather than per constellation. The analysis evaluates the core correlation-length signature, directional anisotropy, orbital-velocity coupling, CMB-frame alignment, and ionospheric/geometric robustness controls. The correlation length and ionospheric persistence are observed; the longitude-matched anisotropy is not robust under spatially clustered resampling. The primary pre-specified monthly λ and EW/NS orbital metrics are null (Bonferroni p > 0.5). A model-free PA-difference metric (mean EW minus NS phase alignment) gives a strong orbital association across 16 chronological full months, but it is exploratory within this MGEX study because it was adopted after the primary fits proved unstable. The direct CMB-projected association is consistent with that signal. The full-sky scan detects directional modulation, but orbital velocity constrains only a plane-projected direction, so its nominal sky axis is not uniquely localizable.

4.3 Orbital-Velocity Coupling

The traditional monthly λ and EW/NS ratio do not correlate significantly with Earth's orbital speed or its projection onto the CMB dipole (Bonferroni p > 0.5 for both). These primary pre-specified metrics are degraded by exponential-fit parameter degeneracy on the short 16-month baseline, where bounded non-linear least squares fitting of $A \exp(-r/\lambda) + C_0$ suffers from severe variance inflation with too few monthly points. The monthly means retain January 2025 through April 2026 as 16 separate chronological observations; the one-day May fragment is excluded. A model-free PA-difference metric (monthly mean EW − NS phase alignment) avoids exponential fitting and has a negative association with orbital speed (r = −0.716, p = 0.0018) and with the velocity projection onto the CMB dipole (r = −0.767, p = 0.0005). The physical hypothesis and expected sign were motivated by the earlier TEP-GNSS papers, which reported comparable orbital associations; this is therefore corroborative across products. It remains exploratory within the MGEX analysis because this particular operationalisation was selected after the primary λ and EW/NS estimators proved unstable. It is not included in the family-wise correction applied to confirmatory tests and should be tested prospectively in a longer MGEX series.

4.4 CMB-Frame Alignment

The CMB analysis uses the same PA-difference observable as Section 4.3 and is therefore a directional characterization, not an independent test. With 16 chronological full-month observations, the direct CMB-projection correlation is r = −0.733, computed from daily EW−NS differences averaged equally within each calendar month. This differs from the −0.767 result in Section 4.3, which averages all pair observations within each month (so months are pair-count weighted) before correlating PA difference with the CMB-projected velocity. The latter is the stronger weighted PA-difference result (p = 0.0005). A 37 × 19 scan gives a nominal maximum at RA = 230°, Dec = +60° (r = −0.745), 82.7° from the CMB dipole. This apparent angular discrepancy cannot reject CMB consistency: Earth’s orbital velocity samples essentially one plane, so the scan identifies only a direction’s projection onto that plane, not its full three-dimensional vector. The CMB and nominal maximum projections correlate at r = 0.984, and 61 of 703 grid directions have |r| at least as large as the direct CMB value. The 30° three-dimensional threshold is consequently not an identifiable test in this geometry. The grid detects directional annual modulation (plus-one permutation p = 0.005 from 200 complete-grid shuffles), while the pre-specified CMB projection remains compatible with it. Widening sectors to 60° gives r = −0.791 at another member of the same projection-degenerate family. The short series further limits precision, so this is corroborative CMB consistency, not a unique sky-axis localization.

4.5 Ionospheric and Geometry Controls

The correlation length persists on geomagnetically quiet days ($$\lambda = 1708$$ km, Kp ≤ 2, 712,186 pairs, R² = 0.743). Active days (Kp ≥ 5, 39,093 pairs) give a longer $$\lambda = 1931$$ km (R² = 0.295); the smaller sample increases the variance and the value awaits confirmation with additional active-period data. Storm-excluded days (Kp < 5) give $$\lambda = 1858$$ km (R² = 0.705, 1,788,462 pairs), consistent with the base value. The distance-matched subset yields a comparable $$\lambda = 1889$$ km (R² = 0.599), indicating the signal is not a product of network geometry. Hemispheric $$\lambda$$ differs north to south, as expected from the uneven station distribution; the distance-matched control is the geometry-robust comparison.

4.6 Null Tests

Null tests were run using the same MGEX combined-clock phase-alignment pipeline. Three of the four nulls (temporal shuffle, spatial shuffle, and phase randomisation) collapsed to R² ≤ 0, confirming that the recovered correlation length depends on the true temporal, spatial and phase information. The solar-rotation reassignment null, however, retains R² = 0.50 under the primary parameters. A sensitivity analysis attributes this to a small-sample bin artifact: short-baseline bins at 100–115 km (40 pairs) and 132–152 km (23 pairs) receive extreme coherence values (0.29 and 0.21) under the specific shuffle sequence, producing a spurious short-baseline spike that the exponential fit interprets as structure. Under a robust analysis (minimum baseline 500 km, minimum bin count 50) all four nulls give R² ≤ 0.122, confirming that the distance-decay structure depends on the true temporal, spatial, and phase information and is not a solar-period artifact.
