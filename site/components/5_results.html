4. Results

The analysis comprises 1,827,555 station-pair-day observations drawn from a network of 418 stations over 462 usable days. Because the MGEX product is a single combined solution, the results are reported once rather than per constellation. The analysis evaluates the core correlation-length signature, directional anisotropy, orbital-velocity coupling, annual-phase templates, and ionospheric/geometric robustness controls. The correlation length and ionospheric persistence are observed; the longitude-matched anisotropy is not robust under spatially clustered resampling. The primary pre-specified monthly λ and EW/NS orbital metrics are null (Bonferroni p > 0.5). A model-free PA-difference metric (mean EW minus NS phase alignment) gives a suggestive orbital association across 16 chronological full months that survives autocorrelation-aware corrections for one CMB-labelled projection channel (corrected p = 0.018; AR(1) p = 0.003; circular-shift floor), but it is exploratory within this MGEX study because it was adopted after the primary fits proved unstable. The full-sky scan detects annual directional modulation, but orbital velocity constrains only a plane-projected phase: the nominal sky axis is not uniquely localizable and the CMB template is not uniquely selected.

The dominant systematic on the λ scale is therefore epoch/window composition (~30–40%), with analysis-center processing contributing a further ~±600 km, both well above the ±155 km cluster-robust statistical error of the pooled estimate.

The choice of decay morphology is tested rather than assumed. On identical log-spaced bins and identical SEM weights, six functional forms are compared by AIC: the exponential (AIC = 1,664.7, $$\lambda = 1{,}862$$ km), a stretched exponential with free exponent (AIC = 1,665.2; the fit selects $$b = 0.98$$, i.e. the pure exponential is recovered rather than assumed), the algebraic suppressor family $$A/[1+(r/r_t)^k]+C_0$$ with free exponent (AIC = 1,893.8 at $$k = 1.78$$), the same family at the derived Paper-0 pairwise exponents $$k=4$$ (AIC = 4,209.0) and $$k=4/3$$ (AIC = 2,125.1), and a Gaussian (AIC = 3,355.2). The exponential is the AIC winner and the nested stretched-exponential family independently returns $$b \approx 1$$: the measured profile is a finite-range exponential correlator — the two-point morphology of a screened spatial field — rather than an algebraic tail. The derived suppressor operators $$\mathcal{S}_\Sigma$$ and $$y(s)$$ describe the response as a function of the ambient environment, a different observable; the correlation kernel measured here is the field's spatial covariance, and it is cleanly exponential.

4.3 Orbital-Velocity Coupling

The traditional monthly λ and EW/NS ratio do not correlate significantly with Earth's orbital speed or its projection onto the CMB dipole (Bonferroni p > 0.5 for both). These primary pre-specified metrics are degraded by exponential-fit parameter degeneracy on the short 16-month baseline, where bounded non-linear least squares fitting of $A \exp(-r/\lambda) + C_0$ suffers from severe variance inflation with too few monthly points. The monthly means retain January 2025 through April 2026 as 16 separate chronological observations; the one-day May fragment is excluded. A model-free PA-difference metric (monthly mean EW − NS phase alignment) avoids exponential fitting and has a negative association with orbital speed (r = −0.716, p = 0.0018) and with the velocity projection onto the CMB dipole (r = −0.767, p = 0.0005). The nominal p-values assume independent monthly points; under autocorrelation-aware diagnostics the associations are tempered but not erased. With effective degrees of freedom from the residual lag-1 autocorrelation (ρ̂ = 0.40 and 0.29), the corrected values are p = 0.072 (speed) and p = 0.018 (v_proj); matched AR(1) surrogates give p = 0.019 and p = 0.003; the phase-preserving circular-shift null gives p = 0.125 and p = 0.063 — the 1/16 resolution floor for the CMB-projection channel. Leave-one-month-out correlations stay within r = −0.66 to −0.86 for both predictors, so the association is not carried by a single favourable phase alignment. The physically motivated velocity-projection channel is thus the one that survives every correction, while the scalar-speed control is marginal. The physical hypothesis and expected sign were motivated by the earlier TEP-GNSS papers, which reported comparable orbital associations; this is therefore corroborative across products. It remains exploratory within the MGEX analysis because this particular operationalisation was selected after the primary λ and EW/NS estimators proved unstable. It is not included in the family-wise correction applied to confirmatory tests and should be tested prospectively in a longer MGEX series. The raw EW − NS contrast is the estimator most sensitive to any operational asymmetry between the east and west station populations (network density, tracking schedules); two controls price that channel directly. A composition-matched difference — EW − NS of cell means averaged with equal weight over (distance × |latitude|) cells — retains the association (r = −0.586, p = 0.017 vs speed; r = −0.650, p = 0.006 vs v_proj, n = 16), and a uniformly-tracked-station subset (both endpoints above the median pair-day participation) retains it more strongly (r = −0.685, p = 0.003; r = −0.739, p = 0.001; corrected p = 0.029 and AR(1) p = 0.007 for the v_proj channel). The association is therefore not carried by population or scheduling asymmetry between the sectors. Two further diagnostics bound the interpretation. First, the fitted-scale nulls are consistent with estimator dilution rather than signal absence: the monthly λ sequence scatters with CV ≈ 21% about its mean while remaining uncorrelated with the PA-diff sequence (r = −0.01), and the per-month per-sector fits that enter the EW/NS λ ratio carry median propagated ratio errors of ≈ 27% — and one month (2025-08) has a sector fit pinned at the λ upper bound, rendering that month's ratio formally uninformative — while the resulting ratio sequence is uncorrelated even with the fit-free anisotropy it is meant to measure (ratio vs PA-diff r = −0.20, p = 0.45) and remains null after dropping the degenerate month (r = −0.10 vs speed, r = −0.17 vs v_proj). A velocity-driven modulation at the observed contrast amplitude cannot propagate through the fitted decay length on a 16-month baseline — the unfitted contrast is the sensitive channel by construction. Second, a hemisphere decomposition of the same contrast (Step 3.4) returns the signed orbital-speed anticorrelation in all three pair classes — intra-northern (r = −0.45), intra-southern (r = −0.88), and cross-hemisphere (r = −0.49) — with the northern and southern annual sinusoid phases consistent with a common value (Δφ = 22° ± 44°). A hemisphere-local seasonal mechanism would appear in anti-phase between the hemispheres; the observed common phase instead indicates a global heliocentric driver.

4.4 Projection-Degenerate Annual-Direction Test

This analysis uses the same PA-difference observable as Section 4.3 and is therefore a directional characterization, not an independent test of a cosmic rest frame. With 16 chronological full-month observations, the direct CMB-projection correlation is r = −0.733, computed from daily EW−NS differences averaged equally within each calendar month. This differs from the −0.767 result in Section 4.3, which averages all pair observations within each month (so months are pair-count weighted) before correlating PA difference with the CMB-projected velocity. The latter is the stronger weighted PA-difference result (p = 0.0005). A 37 × 19 scan gives a nominal maximum at RA = 230°, Dec = +60° (r = −0.745), 82.7° from the CMB dipole. Earth’s orbital velocity samples essentially one plane, so the scan identifies only a direction’s projection onto that plane, not its full three-dimensional vector. The CMB and nominal maximum projections correlate at r = 0.984, and 61 of 703 grid directions have |r| at least as large as the direct CMB value. The 30° three-dimensional threshold is consequently not an identifiable test in this geometry. The grid detects directional annual modulation (plus-one permutation p = 0.005 from 200 complete-grid shuffles), but it does not select the CMB projection over the many compatible annual templates. Widening sectors to 60° gives r = −0.791 at another member of the same projection-degenerate family. The short series further limits precision, so this is evidence only for exploratory annual directional structure, not a CMB-frame detection or a unique sky-axis localization.

4.5a Product-Error and Atmospheric-Channel Controls

Two mundane channels could in principle mimic a distance-ordered clock correlation; each is evaluated against a computed forward model or a within-product partition rather than by label-permutation nulls alone. The leading channel is shared satellite orbit and clock error projecting into each station's receiver-clock estimate. The forward model (Step 3.2) constructs the expected kernel directly: the pairwise cosine similarity of the stations' satellite-weight vectors, built from real CODE SP3 orbits (122 satellites, 300 s cadence, 97 epochs spanning a full day) under standard elevation weighting and the 513 MGEX station coordinates. The kernel is flat above 0.93 through the 0–2,000 km band where the measured coherence carries most of its structure, decays only on the constellation-footprint scale (0.70 at 3–5,000 km, 0.45 at 5–8,000 km, 0.18 at 8–12,000 km — an effective scale an order of magnitude longer than the observed $$\lambda = 1862$$ km), and is everywhere non-negative whereas the measured profile crosses zero. A shared satellite-error projection can weight this kernel but cannot steepen it, so the geometric product-error channel cannot supply the measured decay.

The remaining mundane candidate is the spatially-correlated atmospheric component — tropospheric delay terms absorbed into the per-station clock estimates. An atmospheric driver is weather-organized rather than distance-organized: synoptic fields are hemisphere-confined, and the large-scale modes (annular modes, jet-stream teleconnections) are coherent along latitude bands over $$\sim 10^4$$ km, not across them. Three within-product controls on true station latitudes (Step 3.3) test this directly. First, refitting the profile within hemisphere classes, the cross-hemisphere subset — pairs that cannot share any synoptic weather — returns the cleanest structure in the archive ($$\lambda = 2101$$ km, R² = 0.769, 655,068 pair-days), against intra-NH $$\lambda = 1308$$ km (R² = 0.653). The intra-SH binned profile likewise decays monotonically (+0.63 to −0.14); its weak exponential-fit quality (R² = 0.032) reflects the long-baseline-dominated coverage of the southern network rather than absent structure. Second, at $$d \gt 4000$$ km, same-latitude-band pairs ($$|\Delta\mathrm{lat}| \lt 10°$$) return mean phase alignment −0.096 while different-band pairs ($$|\Delta\mathrm{lat}| \gt 30°$$) return −0.026 — the opposite ordering of the zonal-teleconnection prediction, and not a distance artifact because the same-band subset is the closer one (median 7,963 vs 9,129 km). Third, the decisive cell: same-band pairs at $$d \gt 8000$$ km — where a zonal atmospheric kernel must retain positive coherence — return mean phase alignment −0.118 on 107,013 pair-days, opposite in sign to the atmospheric requirement. The weather-organized component is therefore excluded at the level of its required structure; what remains of the tropospheric channel is per-station incoherent residual error, which cannot generate a distance-ordered pair correlation by construction.

4.5b Estimator Decomposition of the Fitted Scale

The pooled MGEX scale ($$\lambda = 1862$$ km) sits below the IGS-final family reported in Papers 1–2 (3,330–4,549 km). Both analyses measure the same statistic — the cosine of the magnitude-weighted circular mean cross-spectral phase — so the offset can be decomposed estimator-axis by estimator-axis on identical real station-days (Step 3.5). Three documented settings differ between the pipelines: the Welch segment length (96 samples here, $$\Delta f \approx 34.7$$ µHz, versus a full-day segment in Papers 1–2, $$\Delta f \approx 11.6$$ µHz), the per-epoch common-mode removal (applied here; not applied in Papers 1–2), and the clock products themselves.

On a 58-day sample spanning the archive (211,012 pair-days), the factorial decomposition returns: canonical settings $$\lambda = 1285 \pm 233$$ km (day-block bootstrap; the full-archive estimate on all 462 usable days is 1,862 km); common-mode removal switched off, $$\lambda = 1368$$ km; full-day spectral resolution with common-mode, $$\lambda = 1363$$ km; and the Papers-1–2 estimator combination (full-day resolution, no common-mode removal), $$\lambda = 1598$$ km. Restricting the full-day spectrum to the resolved MGEX floor ($$\geq 34.7$$ µHz) returns $$\lambda = 1045$$–$$1049$$ km, confirming that the two lowest resolved bins carry the longest-range content — as required if the correlation is band-ordered. The estimator axes thus move the fitted scale over $$1.0$$–$$1.6 \times 10^3$$ km on identical data (≲±25% around the canonical value) and cannot supply the ~2× offset to the IGS-final family; the residual resides in the clock products themselves — their multi-GNSS combined construction, station complement, and epoch coverage — rather than in the correlation estimator.

The same ordering is recovered per sub-band: on the full-day matrix the fitted scale declines from $$\lambda \approx 1.8$$–$$2.0 \times 10^3$$ km below 30 µHz to $$\approx 0.5$$–$$1.2 \times 10^3$$ km above 50 µHz, mirroring the IGS-final profile, which declines from ≈3.9–5.9×103 km in the tidal bands to ≈1.0–1.4×103 km at intermediate frequencies (Paper 1 §3.5.1). Both products therefore return the same band-ordered, distance-structured correlation; the MGEX profile is a scaled-down version of the IGS-final one. The defensible cross-product statement is a thousands-of-km coherence scale with a shared band ordering. The fitted scale is product- and estimator-conditioned (product-level values ~1.0–4.8×103 km; raw-SPP channels ~0.7–1.1×103 km and the raw ionofree channel returns 4,767 ± 835 km; Paper 3), and no point-scale identification with the geometric saturation radius is made.

Two provenance details are bounded by the same diagnostic. First, GRG product-days are natively 30-s sampled, so on the configured 5-min-labelled frequency grid those station-days resolve a ~0.35–5 mHz band rather than the nominal 10–500 µHz band; substituting their correctly decimated contribution moves the pooled scale by ~1% (1,271 → 1,285 km on the sampled day set; 9 of 486 days carry GRG data). The per-center consequence is larger — the GRG-only matched-day scale moves from 3,257 km to 1,933 km under the corrected pairwise epoch intersection (Step 3.1) — because the per-center path additionally truncated unequal-length series without timestamp alignment, a fault the shared-grid construction removes. The pairing step now thins finer-cadence products to the 300 s grid before spectral estimation. Second, single-window realisations remain epoch-dependent at the ~30–40% level, as reported in §4.1.

The fitted amplitude A decomposes along the same axes (Step 3.6; 46 COD days, 190,867 pair-days, all statistics evaluated on identical pair sets). The phase-alignment index is bounded in [−1, +1] and saturates once the in-phase component dominates the band-integrated cross-spectrum, so A is not a linear estimator of the shared coherent power: on the same bins the canonical configuration returns A = 0.789 while the band-averaged real coherency — the linear in-phase coherence measure — returns A = 0.250. The same statistic-level inflation is visible inside the corpus on Paper 3's raw-SPP analysis, which reports both statistics on identical pair-days: normalised coherence amplitude 0.182 against phase-alignment 1.056, a ~5.8× spread on 61.8 million pairs. Estimator conditioning contributes the next factor: under the Papers-1–2 settings on the same MGEX station-days (single full-day Welch segment, no common-mode removal, no flat-line rejection) the fitted amplitude falls to A = 0.466, and the flat-line rejection alone moves A = 0.436 → 0.789 — held-constant clocks constitute 11.5% of COD and 18.2% of GRG station-days and inject near-random pair phases. Per-product ordering inside MGEX confirms the remaining conditioning: on identical canonical settings GRG returns A = 0.462 against COD 0.789. A labelled transfer calibration (Step 3.6; synthetic red-noise pairs, estimator calibration only) inverts the published amplitudes to underlying band coherences of ρ ≈ 0.30 (MGEX) and ρ ≈ 0.05 (IGS finals) under each family's own spectral window — a product-level difference in the shared in-phase budget — not yet modelled from product construction, and not in contradiction with the measured structure. The fitted offset shows the same bookkeeping: all three product families return negative C₀, and the deeper MGEX asymptote is the zero-sum signature of its per-epoch median datum — toggling the common-mode subtraction on identical pair-days moves C₀ from −0.015 to −0.066, the same distant-pair anticorrelation produced by the conditioned datum kernel (Paper 1, §4.6). The cross-product comparison is therefore carried by the signed, distance-ordered in-phase decay and its scale ordering, not by amplitude equality, and the ~7× headline ratio is a property of the statistic's saturation plus product and estimator conditioning.

4.6 Null Tests

Null tests were run using the same MGEX combined-clock phase-alignment pipeline. Three of the four nulls (temporal shuffle, spatial shuffle, and phase randomisation) collapsed to R² ≤ 0, confirming that the recovered correlation length depends on the true temporal, spatial and phase information. The solar-rotation reassignment null, however, retains R² = 0.50 under the primary parameters. A sensitivity analysis attributes this to a small-sample bin artifact: short-baseline bins at 100–115 km (40 pairs) and 132–152 km (23 pairs) receive extreme coherence values (0.29 and 0.21) under the specific shuffle sequence, producing a spurious short-baseline spike that the exponential fit interprets as structure. Under a robust analysis (minimum baseline 500 km, minimum bin count 50) all four nulls give R² ≤ 0.122, confirming that the distance-decay structure depends on the true temporal, spatial, and phase information and is not a solar-period artifact.
